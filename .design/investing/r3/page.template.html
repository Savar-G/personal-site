Investing

I bought my first stocks in early 2020. I wasn't old enough to open an investment account, so I used my parents'. The money was everything I'd saved from a minimum wage job.

Then COVID hit. In a matter of months, I watched about 90% of it disappear.

That loss sent me to learn from the greats. I read The Intelligent Investor by Benjamin Graham and The Snowball, and I went through everything Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger taught, including every Berkshire Hathaway letter to shareholders. I read every Amazon letter too, and that is where my obsession with consumer products and building great ones started.

Along the way I fell in love with great businesses like Visa, Mastercard, Amazon, and Google. I wanted to understand their moats, and how they built products people use every day.

For a while I planned to go into equity analysis. It came naturally to me. I decided against it, because I believe AI is the most revolutionary technology in human history. A lot of what equity analysts and investment bankers do today is repetitive work that AI will take over.

I'd rather be building with AI than competing against it. Product is where I get to do that: start with the customer, work backwards, and build the kind of products I spent years admiring in those businesses.

Today I have two accounts. My TFSA holds about a dozen businesses I picked myself. My RRSP holds two ETFs: the S&P 500, and a global small cap value fund.
